## In one sentence

Section 10.5 of OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, "Integer Exponents and Scientific Notation": the
negative index defined as a reciprocal, $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$ for a positive integer $n$
and $a \ne 0$, found by subtracting indices in $\frac{x^2}{x^5}$ regardless of which is
larger; the quotient rule collapsed to one form, $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$, and all seven
index laws restated for integer indices and used to simplify expressions such as
$(2x^{-6}y^8)(-5x^5y^{-3}) = -\frac{10y^5}{x}$; then scientific notation, $a \times 10^n$,
converted to and from decimal form and multiplied and divided.

## What it covers

Cite it for these claims, and for no others:

- The quotient rule restated in its two whole-number forms from section 10.4, for a real
  $a \ne 0$ and whole numbers $m$, $n$: $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ for $m > n$ and
  $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = \frac{1}{a^{n-m}}$ for $n > m$. Serves
  [[Index laws and fractional powers]], as the ground this section builds on.
- The negative index found by subtracting indices "regardless of which is larger":
  $\frac{x^2}{x^5} = x^{2-5} = x^{-3}$, while "dividing out common factors" gives
  $\frac{1}{x^3}$, so $x^{-3} = \frac{1}{x^3}$, "and it leads us to the definition of a
  negative exponent". Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]] and
  [[Equivalent fractions and cancellation]].
- The negative index stated as a definition: "If $n$ is a positive integer and $a \ne 0$, then
  $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$." In words, "taking the reciprocal of the base and then changing
  the sign of the exponent"; and the house rule that "Any expression that has negative
  exponents is not considered to be in simplest form", so every answer is rewritten "with
  only positive exponents". Examples $4^{-2} = \frac{1}{16}$, $10^{-3} = \frac{1}{1000}$ and
  $x^{-6} = \frac{1}{x^6}$. Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]] and, for the condition
  $a \ne 0$, [[Division restrictions]].
- "The negative in the exponent does not affect the sign of the base": $(-3)^{-2}
  = \frac{1}{(-3)^2} = \frac{1}{9}$, where the index applies to $-3$, but $-3^{-2}$ "means
  'find the opposite of $3^{-2}$'", rewritten as $-1 \cdot 3^{-2} = -1 \cdot \frac{1}{3^2}
  = -\frac{1}{9}$. Serves [[Powers and radicals]] and
  [[Signed arithmetic and order of operations]].
- Brackets decide what a negative index applies to, under the order of operations, "Do
  exponents before multiplication": $4 \cdot 2^{-1} = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2^1} = 2$ but
  $(4 \cdot 2)^{-1} = 8^{-1} = \frac{1}{8}$; $5y^{-1} = 5 \cdot \frac{1}{y^1} = \frac{5}{y}$ but
  $(5y)^{-1} = \frac{1}{5y}$; and $(-5y)^{-1} = \frac{1}{-5y} = -\frac{1}{5y}$, using
  $\frac{a}{-b} = -\frac{a}{b}$. Serves [[Powers and radicals]],
  [[Signed arithmetic and order of operations]] and [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- The quotient rule in one form once negative indices exist: "the Quotient Property of
  Exponents needs only one form, $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$, where $a \ne 0$ and $m$ and $n$
  are integers"; a larger index below gives a negative index, which is then rewritten by the
  definition. Example $\frac{r^5}{r^{-4}} = r^{5-(-4)} = r^9$, with the warning "Be careful to
  subtract $5 - (-4)$". Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]] and, for subtracting a
  negative, [[Signed arithmetic and order of operations]].
- The laws extended to all integers: "All the exponent properties we developed earlier in
  this chapter with whole number exponents apply to integer exponents, too." The summary, for
  real $a$, $b$ and integers $m$, $n$: $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$, $(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$,
  $(ab)^m = a^m b^m$, $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ with $a \ne 0$, $a^0 = 1$ with $a \ne 0$,
  $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^m = \frac{a^m}{b^m}$ with $b \ne 0$, and $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$,
  the last listed without a restriction in the summary though the boxed definition has
  $a \ne 0$. Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- The product rule with negative indices, "The bases are the same, so add the exponents":
  $x^{-4} \cdot x^6 = x^2$, $y^{-6} \cdot y^4 = y^{-2} = \frac{1}{y^2}$ and
  $z^{-5} \cdot z^{-3} = z^{-8} = \frac{1}{z^8}$. Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- Multiplying monomials with negative indices by reordering so that "like bases" come
  together, adding indices for each base, then taking reciprocals:
  $(m^4 n^{-3})(m^{-5} n^{-2}) = m^{-1} \cdot n^{-5} = \frac{1}{mn^5}$; with coefficients,
  "we multiply the coefficients", $(2x^{-6}y^8)(-5x^5y^{-3}) = 2(-5) \cdot (x^{-6}x^5)
  \cdot (y^8y^{-3}) = -10 \cdot x^{-1} \cdot y^5 = -\frac{10y^5}{x}$. Serves
  [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- Power of a power and power of a product with negative indices: $(k^3)^{-2} = k^{3(-2)}
  = k^{-6} = \frac{1}{k^6}$, which the text labels "the Product to a Power Property,
  $(ab)^m = a^m b^m$" though the law applied is $(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$, a slip in the
  source that a Note must not copy; and $(5x^{-3})^2 = 5^2 (x^{-3})^2 = 25x^{-6}
  = \frac{25}{x^6}$. Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- Scientific notation defined: a number is in scientific notation when it has the form
  $a \times 10^n$ with $a \ge 1$, $a < 10$ and $n$ an integer; $4000 = 4 \times 10^3$ and
  $0.004 = 4 \times \frac{1}{10^3} = 4 \times 10^{-3}$, so a negative index of $10$ is a
  number between $0$ and $1$. The conversion procedures both ways, by moving the decimal
  point and counting places, $37{,}000 = 3.7 \times 10^4$, $0.0052 = 5.2 \times 10^{-3}$,
  $6.2 \times 10^3 = 6200$ and $8.9 \times 10^{-2} = 0.089$; and multiplying and dividing in
  it with the product and quotient rules, $(4 \times 10^5)(2 \times 10^{-7}) = 8 \times 10^{-2}
  = 0.08$ and $\frac{9 \times 10^3}{3 \times 10^{-2}} = 3 \times 10^5 = 300{,}000$.
  **No Node needs scientific notation**: the Anchor Graph has no Node for it, and a Note
  should not cite the section for it. It is listed here so that nobody cites it for that by
  mistake, and so that the author can see what the section holds if a Node ever does need it.

The section has **no fractional powers**: every index in it is an integer, the laws are
stated "for integer exponents" and nothing in it raises anything to $\frac{1}{2}$ or any other
fraction; a Note must not cite it for a fractional index, a root or a radical. It also has no
power of a quotient with a negative index, $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n}$, in its text or
exercises. It also holds a readiness quiz ("Be Prepared") on place value, naming a decimal
and $5 - (-3)$, three "Media" links to videos, the numbered exercise set (including sums of
negative powers such as $2^{-3} + 2^{-2}$, which the text never discusses), "Everyday Math"
exercises on $1.5$ trillion calories, a $0.000125$-day difference between calendar and
astronomical years and two calculator displays in scientific notation, writing exercises and
a self-check. No Node needs them, so no Note should cite the section for them.

## Notation it uses

Each entry below records how this source writes the convention, attributed to it:

- [[Conventions#Index and power]]: *exponent* for the index and *base* for what is raised,
  "the base that is raised to each exponent"; *negative exponent*, *integer exponents* and
  *positive exponents* for the kinds of index; "the exponent, $n$, on the factor $10$";
  *exponential form* for a power of ten left as $10^3$; and *the properties of exponents*,
  also *the exponent properties*, for what this Wiki calls the index laws. The index is a
  *positive integer* in the negative-index definition and *integers* in the summary.
- [[Conventions#Index laws by name]]: *Negative Exponent* as a definition, "the definition of
  a negative exponent" and, in the summary, *Definition of Negative Exponent*,
  $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$; *the Quotient Property of Exponents* in one form for integers,
  $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$; and the summary's seven names, *Product Property*, *Power
  Property*, *Product to a Power Property*, *Quotient Property*, *Zero Exponent Property*,
  *Quotient to a Power Property* and *Definition of Negative Exponent*, with *Summary of
  Exponent Properties* as its heading and *the Properties of Exponents* collectively. The
  product rule is written with a centred dot, $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$, as in sections 10.2
  and 10.4.
- [[Conventions#Multiplication sign]]: a centred dot between numbers and between a number and
  a power, $4 \cdot 2^{-1}$, $-1 \cdot 3^{-2}$ and $4 \cdot 2 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 10^{-7}$, and
  between powers of letters, $m^{-1} \cdot n^{-5}$; brackets as the multiplication sign,
  $(m^4n^{-3})(m^{-5}n^{-2})$ and $2(-5)$; $\times$ between the two factors of a number in
  scientific notation, $3.7 \times 10^4$, with the remark "It is customary in scientific
  notation to use $\times$ as the multiplication sign, even though we avoid using this sign
  elsewhere in algebra"; and no sign between a number and a letter or between letters, $5y$,
  $25x^{-6}$ and $m^4n^{-3}$, with the number written first.
- [[Conventions#Division sign and quotient]]: a quotient written as a stacked fraction,
  $\frac{r^5}{r^{-4}}$ and $\frac{9 \times 10^3}{3 \times 10^{-2}}$, with *Simplify* or
  *Divide* as the instruction; $\div$ does not appear.
- [[Conventions#Brackets]]: it writes *parentheses* for $(\,)$.
- [[Conventions#Order of operations]]: "Remember to always follow the order of operations",
  "Do exponents before multiplication" and "Simplify inside the parentheses first", naming no
  mnemonic.
- [[Conventions#Decimal point and digit groups]]: a decimal point, $0.004$, $0.0052$, $5.2$
  and $0.089$; a comma between digit groups, $37{,}000$, $10{,}000$, $300{,}000$ and
  $2{,}598{,}960$.
- [[Conventions#Division restriction]]: the excluded value written after a comma in each boxed
  statement, $a \ne 0$ and $b \ne 0$; no prose word for it, neither *non-zero* nor *nonzero*,
  appears in the section.
- [[Conventions#Cancelling common factors]]: *dividing out common factors*, with the
  factors crossed out in red in a figure whose `alt` text reads "Two x's are crossed out in
  red on the top and on the bottom"; the word *cancel* does not appear.
- [[Conventions#The negative of an expression]]: *the opposite*, "find the opposite of
  $3^{-2}$", for the minus in $-3^{-2}$, with "Rewrite as a product with $-1$",
  $-1 \cdot 3^{-2}$; and the sign of a fraction moved to the front by
  $\frac{a}{-b} = -\frac{a}{b}$.
- [[Conventions#Commutative law]]: "Use the Commutative Property to get like bases together"
  and "Use the Commutative Property to rearrange the factors", capitalised and unqualified,
  for reordering a product.
- [[Conventions#Term, coefficient and constant]]: *monomial* for a one-term expression, a
  word this Wiki has not adopted, and *numerical coefficients*, "If the monomials have
  numerical coefficients, we multiply the coefficients", for the numbers $2$ and $-5$ in
  $(2x^{-6}y^8)(-5x^5y^{-3})$.
- [[Conventions#Absolute value]]: $|n|$ for the size of an integer regardless of sign, "move
  the decimal point $|n|$ places to the left", used in one procedure and never named.

*Simplify* is its instruction for applying the laws and for working out a number, $4^{-2}$ to
$\frac{1}{16}$; *Write in scientific notation*, *Convert to decimal form* and *Multiply* or
*Divide. Write answers in decimal form* are its instructions for scientific notation. It
says *decimal notation* and *decimal form* interchangeably for an ordinary numeral. The
reciprocal is written as a stacked fraction with $1$ on top, $\frac{1}{a^n}$, and the word
*reciprocal* is used for it; a negative quotient carries its sign in front of the fraction,
$-\frac{1}{9}$. A check of a conversion is written as a chain of equal values,
$5.2 \times 10^{-3}$, $5.2 \times \frac{1}{1000}$, $5.2 \times 0.001$, $0.0052$, with no
question mark over the equals sign. Variables are single italic letters, and the bases in the
laws are $a$, $b$ with indices $m$, $n$. Its worked solutions for the quotient example and
the decimal-point moves are images, with the expression described in the image's `alt` text.
It spells in American English (*favorite*), which is spelling, not notation: a Note citing it
still writes British English.
