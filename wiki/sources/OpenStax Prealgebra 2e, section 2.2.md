## In one sentence

Section 2.2 of OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, "Evaluate, Simplify, and Translate Expressions":
evaluating an expression by substituting a number for its variable and following the order of
operations, naming the terms and coefficients of an expression, picking out like terms, and
simplifying by adding their coefficients, then translating word phrases into expressions.

## What it covers

Cite it for these claims, and for no others:

- To evaluate an expression is to find its value when the variable is replaced by a given
  number: substitute the number for the variable, then simplify by the order of operations.
  $x + 7$ is $10$ when $x = 3$ and $19$ when $x = 12$, so the value varies with the value
  used for the variable. Serves [[Variables, substitution, and brackets]].
- $9x$ means $9$ times $x$, so evaluating $9x - 2$ at $x = 5$ gives $9 \cdot 5 - 2 = 43$;
  both the dot and brackets, $9(1)$, mean multiply. Serves
  [[Variables, substitution, and brackets]].
- Substituting into a power, $x^2$ at $x = 10$ and $2^x$ at $x = 5$, and into an expression
  with two variables, $3x + 4y - 6$ at $x = 10$, $y = 2$. Serves
  [[Variables, substitution, and brackets]].
- $2x^2$ means $2 \cdot x \cdot x$ and differs from $(2x)^2$, which means $2x \cdot 2x$; so
  $2x^2 + 3x + 8$ at $x = 4$ is $2 \cdot 16 + 12 + 8 = 52$. Serves
  [[Variables, substitution, and brackets]].
- A term is a constant or the product of a constant and one or more variables; the
  coefficient is the constant that multiplies the variables, $9$ in $9a$, and $1$ in $y$
  since $y = 1 \cdot y$; the coefficient of a constant term is the constant itself. The
  operation before a term goes with it: $2x + 7y + 4$ has terms $2x$, $7y$, $4$. Serves
  [[Variables, substitution, and brackets]].
- Like terms are terms that are constants or have the same variables with the same exponents:
  among $5x, 7, n^2, 4, 3x, 9n^2$, the pairs $7$ and $4$, $5x$ and $3x$, $n^2$ and $9n^2$.
  $9x$ and $8xy$ have no like term where no other term has exactly their variables. Serves
  [[Distributive law, expansion, and like terms]].
- Simplifying by combining like terms: $3x + 6x = 9x$ because three $x$'s and six more
  make nine; rearrange by the commutative property of addition so like terms sit together,
  then add their coefficients: $3x + 7 + 4x + 5 = 7x + 12$ and
  $7x^2 + 8x - x^2 - 4x = 6x^2 + 4x$, which is in simplest form because $6x^2$ and $4x$ are
  not like terms. Serves [[Distributive law, expansion, and like terms]].

The section also translates word phrases into expressions (*the difference of*, *less
than*, *five times the sum of $m$ and $n$* as $5(m + n)$) with its Table 2.7 of phrases for
the four operations, writes expressions for a window's height and for a number of dimes, and
holds a readiness quiz ("Be Prepared"), "Everyday Math" exercises on insurance deductibles,
the numbered exercise set, a video link and a self-check. No Node needs them, so no Note
should cite the section for them.

## Notation it uses

Each entry below records how this source writes the convention, attributed to it:

- [[Conventions#Order of operations]]: "following the order of operations", and "Add and
  subtract left to right", naming no mnemonic.
- [[Conventions#Implied multiplication after division]]: it offers $a \div b$, $a/b$ and
  $\frac{a}{b}$ as the same quotient, and writes $10x \div 3$, $10x/3$ and $\frac{10x}{3}$
  with the product before the sign, never after it.
- [[Conventions#Brackets]]: it writes *parentheses* for $(\,)$.
- [[Conventions#Index and power]]: it writes *exponent* for the index, "the variable is an
  exponent" in $2^x$, and "raised to the power of $1$".
- [[Conventions#Multiplication sign]]: no sign between a number and a letter, $9x$, and the
  number first; a centred dot between numbers, $9 \cdot 5$ and $2 \cdot x \cdot x$; and
  brackets as a multiplication sign, $9(1)$, $a(b)$ and $(a)(b)$, which the Wiki's line does
  not speak to.
- [[Conventions#Decimal point and digit groups]]: it groups digits with commas, $2{,}100$
  and $19{,}400$; no decimal number appears.
- [[Conventions#Like terms]]: it writes *combine like terms*, defines them as "terms that are
  either constants or have the same variables with the same exponents", and the step as "add
  the coefficients of the like terms".
- [[Conventions#Commutative law]]: it writes *the Commutative Property of Addition*,
  capitalised.
- [[Conventions#Term, coefficient and constant]]: *term*, *coefficient* and *constant*, with
  *constant term* for a term with no variable.
- [[Conventions#Evaluate and substitute]]: "Evaluate $x + 7$ when $x = 3$", and "substitute
  $3$ for $x$"; the result "has a value of $10$".

Variables are single italic letters and fractions are stacked, $\frac{a}{b}$. It spells in
American English (*meters*), which is spelling, not notation: a Note citing it still writes
British English.
