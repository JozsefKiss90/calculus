## In one sentence

Section 9.7 of OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, "Solve a Formula for a Specific Variable": using the
formula $d = rt$ with numbers, then rearranging $d = rt$, $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$, $I = Prt$,
$3x + 2y = 18$ and $P = a + b + c$ so that one chosen letter stands alone on one side, by the
same steps used to solve an equation with numbers, treating the other letters as if they were
numbers.

## What it covers

Cite it for these claims, and for no others:

- A formula relates letters that each stand for a quantity, $d = rt$ with $d$ for distance,
  $r$ for rate and $t$ for time; substituting the known values and solving gives the unknown
  one, $520 = 65t$ so $t = 8$. The units multiply and cancel like numbers: miles per hour
  times hours gives miles, and dividing miles by miles per hour gives hours. Serves
  [[Ratios, proportion, units, and average speed]] and
  [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- What rearranging a formula means: "To solve a formula for a specific variable means to get
  that variable by itself with a coefficient of $1$ on one side of the equation and all the
  other variables and constants on the other side." The result is another formula, made only
  of letters, that gives the chosen quantity directly whenever the others are known. Serves
  [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- Rearranging uses the same steps as solving with numbers, which the section shows by working
  a numerical case and the general case side by side: $520 = 65t$ is divided by $65$ to give
  $t = 8$, and $d = rt$ is divided by $r$ to give $t = \frac{d}{r}$. Serves
  [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- A fractional coefficient is cleared first by multiplying both sides: $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$
  becomes $2A = bh$, then $h = \frac{2A}{b}$; with numbers, $90 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 15 \cdot h$
  becomes $180 = 15h$, so $h = 12$. Serves [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- A product of several letters is treated as one coefficient: $I = Prt$ is divided by $rt$ to
  give $P = \frac{I}{rt}$, as $5600 = P(0.28)$ is divided by $0.28$ to give $P = 20\,000$.
  Serves [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- A letter that is added is removed by subtracting it from both sides, and the result may
  hold other letters: $P = a + b + c$ gives $a = P - b - c$; $3x + y = 10$ gives
  $y = 10 - 3x$. Serves [[Equations and rearranging formulas]] and [[Inverse operations]].
- An equation in $x$ and $y$ is rearranged for $y$ by first isolating the $y$-term and then
  dividing by its coefficient: $3x + 2y = 18$ gives $2y = 18 - 3x$ and
  $y = \frac{18 - 3x}{2}$; $6x + 5y = 13$ gives $y = \frac{13 - 6x}{5}$. With $x = 4$ the
  same steps give $y = 3$. Serves [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- Checking a solution of a formula: substitute the numbers into the formula and make sure the
  result is a true statement, $520 = 65 \cdot 8$, so $520 = 520$. Serves
  [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].

The section also holds a readiness quiz ("Be Prepared"), the seven-step word-problem strategy
(read, identify, name, translate, solve, check, answer) applied to distance problems, the
geometry and money formulas themselves (the area of a triangle, simple interest, perimeter
$P = 2L + 2W$, circumference $C = \pi d$, volume $V = LWH$ and the temperature conversions in
"Everyday Math"), a "Links to Literacy" activity, links to videos, the numbered exercise set,
writing exercises and a self-check. No Node needs them, so no Note should cite the section
for them. Its running-text mathematics is short; the step-by-step working of most examples
is in images, with the working repeated in each image's `alt` text.

## Notation it uses

Each entry below records how this source writes the convention, attributed to it:

- [[Conventions#Multiplication sign]]: no sign between letters, $rt$, $bh$, $Prt$ and $LWH$,
  and none between a number and a letter, $65t$, $15h$ and $2A$, with a fractional
  coefficient stacked before the letters, $\frac{1}{2}bh$. A centred dot between numbers,
  $12 \cdot 3\frac{1}{2}$ and $65 \cdot 8$, and between letters when it names the step,
  "Multiply $r \cdot t$"; brackets as the multiplication sign when a number is substituted,
  $3(4) + 2y$ and $P(0.28)$, and around a product treated as one factor, $I = P(rt)$, in its
  images; and a letter $x$ for *times* in a video title, "Distance = Rate x Time".
- [[Conventions#Decimal point and digit groups]]: a decimal point, $0.04$, $0.28$ and $3.5$;
  a comma between digit groups in money amounts, $\$5{,}600$ and $\$20{,}000$, and in the
  answer line of an image, $20{,}000 = P$, while the same amount in the equation is $5600$.
- [[Conventions#Term, coefficient and constant]]: "with a coefficient of $1$", "the $y$-term"
  and "the term with $y$", "Divide $5$ to make the coefficient $1$", and "all the other
  variables and constants".
- [[Conventions#Evaluate and substitute]]: "Substitute in the given information", "Substitute
  any given values", and *when* for the values taken, "when $d = 520$ and $r = 65$"; never
  *at*.
- [[Conventions#Equation, solution and solve]]: *solve the formula for $t$*, *isolate* ("We
  will isolate $a$ on one side of the equation"), "the formula $t = \frac{d}{r}$ is solved
  for $t$", and *solving a literal equation*; *equation* and *formula* used interchangeably
  for $3x + y = 10$; a *check* that substitutes "the numbers into the formula" until "the
  result is a true statement", written in its MathML with a question mark over the equals
  sign, $520 \overset{?}{=} 65 \cdot 8$, and a tick after the last line.
- [[Conventions#Properties of equality]]: it names no property here, only the step: "Subtract
  $b$ and $c$ from both sides to isolate $a$", "Subtract $3x$ from both sides", "Divide to
  isolate $t$", "Clear the fractions".
- [[Conventions#Formula and its subject]]: *solve the formula for $h$*, never *make $h$ the
  subject*; a *formula* is "made up only of variables", whose letters it also calls
  *literals*; and *in general* for the rearranged formula against *when* for a numerical case.
- [[Conventions#Speed and rate]]: *rate* for speed throughout, "a uniform (constant) rate",
  "a steady rate of $65$ miles per hour", and "mph" in its mini-charts, with $r$ for it in
  $d = rt$; *speed* appears once, in an exercise ("What was the speed of the train?").

Fractions are stacked, $\frac{1}{2}$, mixed numbers are written as a whole number against a
fraction, $3\frac{1}{2}$ hours, and variables are single italic letters, including capitals
$A$, $P$, $I$, $L$, $W$, $H$ for named quantities. Percentages are written with the sign,
$4\%$, and converted to a decimal, $0.04$, before use. It spells in American English
(*traveled*, *summarize*), which is spelling, not notation: a Note citing it still writes
British English.
