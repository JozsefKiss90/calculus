## In one sentence

Section 7.3 of OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, "Distributive Property": the law
$a(b + c) = ab + ac$ and its two other forms, used to remove brackets from expressions with
whole, fractional, decimal, variable and negative multipliers, then checked by evaluating both
forms at a value.

## What it covers

Cite it for these claims, and for no others:

- The distributive law, stated for real numbers $a$, $b$, $c$ as $a(b + c) = ab + ac$, with
  its two other forms $a(b - c) = ab - ac$ and $(b + c)a = ba + ca$. Serves
  [[Distributive law, expansion, and like terms]].
- Expanding to remove brackets, $3(x + 4) = 3x + 12$, because $x$ and $4$ are not like terms
  and so cannot be added inside the brackets first. Serves
  [[Distributive law, expansion, and like terms]].
- Expanding with a fraction, a decimal or a variable as the multiplier:
  $\frac{3}{4}(n + 12)$, $100(0.3 + 0.25q)$, $m(n - 4) = mn - 4m$. Serves
  [[Distributive law, expansion, and like terms]].
- Expanding with a negative multiplier, $-2(4y + 1) = -8y - 2$, and the negative of a
  bracket as multiplication by $-1$: $-(y + 5) = -y - 5$ because $-a = -1 \cdot a$. Serves
  [[Distributive law, expansion, and like terms]].
- Expanding as a step in the order of operations and then collecting like terms,
  $8 - 2(x + 3) = 2 - 2x$ and $4(x - 8) - (x + 3) = 3x - 35$. Serves
  [[Distributive law, expansion, and like terms]].
- Checking an expansion by substituting one value into both forms, $6(5y + 1)$ and
  $6 \cdot 5y + 6 \cdot 1$ at $y = 10$, and finding they agree; and writing the number before
  the letter in a term, $m \cdot 4$ as $4m$, by commutativity. Serves
  [[Variables, substitution, and brackets]].

## Notation it uses

Each entry below records how this source writes the convention, attributed to it:

- [[Conventions#Multiplication sign]]: a centred dot between numbers, $3 \cdot x + 3 \cdot 4$
  and $6 \cdot 5y$, and a negative factor in brackets, $(-2) \cdot 1$ and $(-10)(0.9)$. No sign
  between a number and a letter, $3x$, with the number written first. A number or a bracket
  against a bracket with no sign, $3(x + 4)$ and $(x + 8)p$, which the Wiki's line does not
  speak to.
- [[Conventions#Brackets]]: it writes *parentheses* for $(\,)$.
- [[Conventions#Order of operations]]: "the order of operations says to work in the
  parentheses first", naming no mnemonic.
- [[Conventions#Decimal point and digit groups]]: a decimal point, $9.25$; no number long
  enough to group.
- [[Conventions#Distributive law]]: it writes *the Distributive Property*, capitalised, and
  *distribute* for the step that removes brackets.
- [[Conventions#Like terms]]: it writes *combine like terms*.
- [[Conventions#The negative of an expression]]: it writes *the opposite* of an expression for
  $-(y + 5)$.

Fractions are stacked, $\frac{3}{4}$, and variables are single italic letters. It spells in
American English (*favorite*), which is spelling, not notation: a Note citing it still writes
British English.
