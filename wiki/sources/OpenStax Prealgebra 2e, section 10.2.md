## In one sentence

Section 10.2 of OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, "Use Multiplication Properties of Exponents":
exponential notation $a^m$ as $m$ factors of $a$, then the three multiplication laws for
whole-number indices, $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$, $(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$ and
$(ab)^m = a^m b^m$, each found by counting factors, checked with numbers, and combined to
simplify and multiply monomials such as $(6n)^2(4n^3) = 144n^5$.

## What it covers

Cite it for these claims, and for no others:

- Exponential notation: in $a^m$ the $a$ is the base and the $m$ the exponent, "$a^m$ means
  multiply $m$ factors of $a$", read "$a$ to the $m$th power"; $5^3 = 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 = 125$,
  $9^1 = 9$, and a letter with no index written is to the power $1$, $b = b^1$. Serves
  [[Index laws and fractional powers]] and [[Powers and radicals]].
- Squaring a fraction and a decimal by writing out the two factors,
  $\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)^2 = \frac{49}{64}$ and $(0.74)^2 = 0.5476$. Serves
  [[Multiplication, division, squares, and roots]].
- Brackets decide what the index applies to: $(-3)^4 = 81$, four factors of $-3$, but
  $-3^4 = -(3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3) = -81$, where only the $3$ is raised and the result is
  then negated. Serves [[Powers and radicals]] and
  [[Signed arithmetic and order of operations]].
- The product rule, $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$ for a real number $a$ and counting numbers $m$,
  $n$, found by counting factors: $x^2 \cdot x^3$ is two factors of $x$ then three, five in
  all, so $x^{2+3} = x^5$; "To multiply with like bases, add the exponents." Examples
  $x^5 \cdot x^7 = x^{12}$, $b^4 \cdot b = b^5$, $2^7 \cdot 2^9 = 2^{16}$ left as a power, and
  the extension to three factors, $x^3 \cdot x^4 \cdot x^2 = x^9$. Serves
  [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- Power of a power, $(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$ for whole numbers $m$, $n$, found by counting
  factors: $(x^2)^3$ is three groups of two factors, six in all, so $x^{2 \cdot 3} = x^6$; "To
  raise a power to a power, multiply the exponents." Examples $(x^5)^7 = x^{35}$ and
  $(3^6)^8 = 3^{48}$. Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- Power of a product, $(ab)^m = a^m b^m$ for a whole number $m$, found by grouping like
  factors: $(2x)^3 = 2x \cdot 2x \cdot 2x = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x
  = 2^3 \cdot x^3$; "To raise a product to a power, raise each factor to that power."
  Examples $(-11x)^2 = 121x^2$ and $(3xy)^3 = 27x^3y^3$. Serves
  [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- Each law checked with numbers before use: $2^2 \cdot 2^3 \overset{?}{=} 2^{2+3}$,
  $4 \cdot 8 \overset{?}{=} 2^5$, $32 = 32$; $(5^2)^3 = 15\,625 = 5^6$; and
  $(2 \cdot 3)^2 = 36 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2$. Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- Applying several laws in one simplification: $(x^2)^6 (x^5)^4 = x^{12} \cdot x^{20} = x^{32}$;
  $(-7x^3y^4)^2 = (-7)^2 (x^3)^2 (y^4)^2 = 49x^6y^8$; $(6n)^2(4n^3) = 36n^2 \cdot 4n^3
  = 144n^5$, with the remark that an index outside brackets applies to every factor inside
  them, while an index inside applies only to its own base; and
  $(3p^2q)^4(2pq^2)^3 = 648p^{11}q^{10}$. Serves [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- Multiplying monomials by reordering the factors so that the numbers come together and like
  bases come together, then multiplying the numbers and adding the indices:
  $(4x^2)(-5x^3) = 4 \cdot (-5) \cdot x^2 \cdot x^3 = -20x^5$ and
  $\left(\frac{3}{4}c^3d\right)(12cd^2) = 9c^4d^3$. Serves
  [[Index laws and fractional powers]].
- The scope the section itself sets: "All the exponent properties hold true for any real
  numbers, but right now we will only use whole number exponents." Serves
  [[Index laws and fractional powers]], as the ground the later laws extend.

The section has **no fractional powers**, no zero or negative index, no quotient rule
$a^m / a^n$ and no power of a quotient; a Note must not cite it for any of those. It also
holds a readiness quiz ("Be Prepared"), two "Media" links to videos, the numbered exercise
set (including exercises with a letter as the index, $x^a \cdot x^2$ and $(x^2)^y$, which the
text never discusses), "Everyday Math" exercises on a chain email reaching $6^8$ people and
a salary of $40\,000(1.05)^{10}$ dollars, writing exercises and a self-check. No Node needs
them, so no Note should cite the section for them.

## Notation it uses

Each entry below records how this source writes the convention, attributed to it:

- [[Conventions#Index and power]]: *exponent* for the index and *base* for what is raised,
  *exponential notation* for $a^m$ and *exponential expression* for an expression holding
  one; "$a$ to the $m$th power" and "raise a power to a power"; and *the properties of
  exponents* for what this Wiki calls the index laws. The index is a *counting number* in
  the product rule and a *whole number* in the other two laws.
- [[Conventions#Index laws by name]]: *Product Property of Exponents*, *Power Property of
  Exponents* and *Product to a Power Property of Exponents*, capitalised, the last also called
  *the Power of a Product Property* in the same section; collectively *the Properties of
  Exponents*. The laws are written with a centred dot, $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$ and
  $(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$, but $(ab)^m = a^m b^m$ without one.
- [[Conventions#Multiplication sign]]: a centred dot between numbers, $5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5$ and
  $4 \cdot 8$, and between powers and letters, $x^2 \cdot x^3$ and
  $c^3 \cdot c \cdot d \cdot d^2$; brackets as the multiplication sign, $(-3)(-3)(-3)(-3)$,
  $(0.74)(0.74)$, $(4x^2)(-5x^3)$ and $40{,}000(1.05)$; no sign between a number and a letter
  or between letters, $2x$, $121x^2$ and $27x^3y^3$, with the number written first; and an
  asterisk, "x*x", in the `alt` text of two figures only.
- [[Conventions#Brackets]]: it writes *parentheses* for $(\,)$.
- [[Conventions#Order of operations]]: "the parentheses tell us to raise the $(-3)$ to the
  4th power" against "we raise only the $3$ to the 4th power and then find the opposite",
  naming no mnemonic.
- [[Conventions#Decimal point and digit groups]]: a decimal point, $0.74$ and $0.5476$; a
  comma between digit groups, $15{,}625$ and $\$40{,}000$.
- [[Conventions#Like terms]]: "when you combine like terms by adding and subtracting, you
  need to have the same base with the same exponent".
- [[Conventions#The negative of an expression]]: "find the opposite" for the minus in
  $-3^4 = -(3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3)$.
- [[Conventions#Commutative law]]: "Use the Commutative Property to rearrange the factors",
  capitalised and unqualified, for reordering a product.
- [[Conventions#Term, coefficient and constant]]: *constants* for the numerical factors of a
  product, "Multiply the constants and add the exponents" for $36 \cdot 4 \cdot n^2 \cdot n^3$,
  where this Wiki would say coefficients; and *monomial* for a one-term expression such as
  $4x^2$, a word this Wiki has not adopted.
- [[Conventions#Equation, solution and solve]]: a numerical check of a law, not of a solution,
  written with a question mark over the equals sign and a tick after the last line,
  $2^2 \cdot 2^3 \overset{?}{=} 2^{2+3}$, $32 = 32$.

*Simplify* is its one instruction for every task: for working out a number, $5^3$ to $125$,
and for applying the laws, $x^5 \cdot x^7$ to $x^{12}$. Fractions are stacked, $\frac{7}{8}$,
and a fraction raised to a power is bracketed, $\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)^2$. Variables are
single italic letters, and the bases in the laws are $a$, $b$ with indices $m$, $n$. Its
worked solutions put each intermediate step with the index highlighted into an image, with the
expression repeated in the image's `alt` text. It spells in American English (*summarize*),
which is spelling, not notation: a Note citing it still writes British English.
