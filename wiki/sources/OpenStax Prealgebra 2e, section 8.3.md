## In one sentence

Section 8.3 of OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, "Solve Equations with Variables and Constants on
Both Sides": solving a linear equation by doing the same thing to both sides, collecting the
letter terms on one side and the numbers on the other, dividing to make the coefficient $1$,
and checking the solution by substituting it into the original equation, then a general
strategy that first expands brackets and collects like terms.

## What it covers

Cite it for these claims, and for no others:

- Whatever is done to one side of an equation must be done to the other to keep the equality:
  adding, subtracting, multiplying by or dividing by the same number on both sides, which the
  section names the Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication and Division Properties of Equality.
  Serves [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- A term that is out of place is removed by undoing the operation that put it there: in
  $4x + 6 = -14$ the $6$ was added, so subtract $6$ from both sides; in $2y - 7 = 15$ the $7$
  was subtracted, so add $7$ to both sides; and $4x = -20$ is undone by dividing both sides by
  $4$. Serves [[Inverse operations]] and [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- Solving by choosing one side for the letter terms and the other for the numbers, then
  collecting each kind to its side: $5x = 4x + 7$ gives $x = 7$ by subtracting $4x$;
  $7x + 5 = 6x + 2$ gives $x + 5 = 2$ and then $x = -3$; $5y - 8 = 7y$ gives $-8 = 2y$, so
  $y = -4$, rewritten with the letter on the left. Serves
  [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- The five steps for an equation with letters and numbers on both sides: choose the sides,
  collect the letter terms, collect the numbers, make the coefficient of the letter $1$ by
  multiplying or dividing, then check. Choosing as the letter side the one with the larger
  coefficient avoids a negative coefficient, as in $2a - 7 = 5a + 8$, solved as $-15 = 3a$.
  Serves [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- The same steps with fractional and decimal coefficients, $\frac{3}{2}x + 5 = \frac{1}{2}x - 3$
  and $3.4x + 4 = 1.6x - 5$, and with a solution that is a fraction, $y = -\frac{1}{3}$ from
  $-6y = 2$ and $x = \frac{3}{2}$ from $4x = 6$. Serves
  [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- The general strategy for any linear equation: simplify each side first, expanding brackets
  and collecting like terms, before collecting terms across sides; $3(x + 2) = 18$ becomes
  $3x + 6 = 18$, $4(x - 2) + 5 = -3$ becomes $4x - 3 = -3$ with solution $x = 0$, and
  $3(x - 2) - 5 = 4(2x + 1) + 5$ becomes $3x - 11 = 8x + 9$. Serves
  [[Equations and rearranging formulas]], and relies on
  [[Distributive law, expansion, and like terms]] for the expanding step.
- A leading minus on a bracket is expanded with care: $-(x + 5) = 7$ becomes $-x - 5 = 7$, so
  $-x = 12$, and the coefficient is made $1$ by multiplying both sides by $-1$, giving
  $x = -12$; $8 - 2(3y + 5) = 0$ becomes $8 - 6y - 10 = 0$. Serves
  [[Equations and rearranging formulas]].
- Checking a solution: substitute it into the original equation and work out both sides; the
  solution is right when the result is a true statement, $-14 = -14$ for $x = -5$ in
  $4x + 6 = -14$. Serves [[Equations and rearranging formulas]] and
  [[Variables, substitution, and brackets]].
